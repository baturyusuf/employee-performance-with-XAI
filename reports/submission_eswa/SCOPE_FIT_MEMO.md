# Scope fit for Expert Systems with Applications

The manuscript addresses a testing problem for intelligent human-resource prediction systems. A system can lead an aggregate ordinal metric while failing to recognize the highest recorded performance category, and its apparent quality can change with model-selection objective, information availability, calibration measure, explanation test, subgroup support, and target construction.

The contribution is an operational audit protocol rather than a new predictor. It connects explicit information contracts, nested training-only selection, exactly-once held-out predictions, ordinal and class-level evaluation, cross-fitted calibration, exact-model explanations, stability and deletion diagnostics, support-aware subgroup and proxy analysis, and numerical evidence lineage.

This is an ESWA contribution because the journal's official scope includes the design, testing, implementation, and management of intelligent systems, practical guidance for their development, and human-resources management and human capital as application areas. The central contribution concerns intelligent-system evaluation methodology. It is not primarily an organizational-behavior theory paper, a new knowledge-representation method, or a production HR decision system.

The strongest editorial evidence is concrete: quadratic-weighted-kappa selection raises nominal-XGBoost QWK to 0.6418 while highest-rating recall falls to 0.0076; Random Forest also has zero highest-rating recall despite leading the canonical QWK and ordinal-MAE comparison. The manuscript turns this conflict into reusable testing requirements while retaining explicit limits on prospective validity, fairness, causality, and deployment.
